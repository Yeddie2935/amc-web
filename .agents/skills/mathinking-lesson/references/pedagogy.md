# Pedagogy and narrative rules

## Start from a mathematical need

Discovery must precede terminology unless the lesson contract documents a strong reason otherwise. Begin with a concrete object, puzzle, prediction, or strain in an existing method. Let the learner experience the need for the new idea before naming it.

The story should feel like:

`problem -> attempt -> uncertainty -> visible structure -> discovery -> name -> apply`

It should not feel like:

`definition -> worked example -> exercise`

Do not turn the preferred arc into identical lesson templates. Preserve the causal relationship: each beat should create the question answered by the next.

## Write as a tutor with memory

Use concise student language first and mathematical vocabulary second. Refer naturally to shared work:

- “Remember the problem with exactly one zero?”
- “Try the same idea here.”
- “What changed?”
- “That worked because…”
- “Now change just one thing.”

Avoid curriculum narration:

- “The previous lesson established…”
- “Apply the prior strategy…”
- “Consider the analogous condition…”
- “The learner will…”
- mapping instructions, implementation notes, or author rationale.

Put internal explanation in author-only fields such as `purpose`, `expectedRealization`, `whyNow`, or generation notes. Read all rendered fields in sequence to catch leakage.

Keep prose economical. Its job is to connect mathematical experiences, focus attention, and name what the learner has already seen. Do not restate an adequate diagram or animation in paragraph form.

## Let interaction create thought

Prefer structured interaction over blank free response when choosing, sorting, predicting, matching, or manipulating can expose the reasoning. A structured interaction must still require a meaningful mathematical decision; it is not just a disguised “Next” button.

Use free response when formulation itself is the learning goal or reasonable answers cannot be anticipated without destroying the insight.

Do not mistake organizing known examples for proving a universe complete. When completeness matters:

1. distinguish the examples already found from all possible outcomes;
2. show why every possible outcome must enter some group;
3. finish or bound every group;
4. show why no outcome can enter more than one group.

For casework, explain the ideas plainly before naming them:

- every possibility is included, then introduce **exhaustive**;
- nothing is counted twice, then introduce **non-overlapping**;
- each smaller group must be easier to finish.

Apply the same proof discipline outside casework whenever the lesson claims a list, classification, or construction is complete.

## Transfer rather than imitation

“Play with it” should change something meaningful and often asks the learner to choose a representation, split, invariant, or first move. Do not put a generated problem directly before an authentic problem if both ask essentially the same question.

Competition application should feel causally prepared. The prior beat should create the exact question, representation, or strategy that makes the mapped problem natural. Harvest why the strategy worked afterward rather than celebrating only the answer.

Independent practice must require a fresh decision. It may reuse the insight, but not the teaching problem or its surface shell.

## End with consolidation, then tension

Before the closing hook, consolidate:

- what makes the method work;
- what mistakes to avoid;
- when to recognize or use it.

Prefer a compact checklist, small comparison, or lightweight interaction over a summary paragraph.

When a narrative successor exists, change one feature of shared work to expose an unresolved limitation or new question. Do not teach the successor's method. Make the next lesson feel like the same conversation continuing.

## Pedagogical rejection checklist

Revise the lesson if any answer is yes:

- Does terminology arrive before the learner has something to name?
- Could the learner proceed by clicking without making a mathematical decision?
- Does a visual decorate rather than explain the current question?
- Does a one-line green answer replace a worked teaching resolution?
- Is the mapped authentic problem an interruption rather than a consequence?
- Does a predecessor callback merely mention a prior lesson without using its representation?
- Does the ending open a new topic before consolidating the current one?
